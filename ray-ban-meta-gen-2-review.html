Ray-Ban Meta Gen 2: one of the most complete consumer AI-glasses options right now

Content updated: 10 September 2026 · About these dates

Not yet scored Rating approach

Ray-Ban Meta Gen 2 combines a camera, open-ear audio and Meta AI in a familiar glasses design. The meaningful hardware upgrades over Gen 1 are higher-resolution video and longer battery life; a September firmware update also makes navigation and audio more useful. Privacy, regional AI availability and price still deserve careful consideration.

✓ Pros

  • Hands-free point-of-view capture
  • Open-ear audio
  • Voice-based Meta AI functions
  • Longer rated battery life than Gen 1
  • Newer firmware adds hands-free walking navigation in supported locations

✕ Cons

  • AI features, languages and navigation availability vary by country
  • Less filming control than dedicated cameras
  • Frame and prescription choices affect fit and price
  • Camera wearables require care around other people’s privacy

Research status: AI Gear Hub has not physically tested this pair. This review is based on current official Ray-Ban/Meta product information and dated public reporting. Prices, firmware features and regional AI availability can change.

Best for

Hands-free capture + everyday AI

Useful for creators, travelers and everyday users who want POV photos/video, open-ear audio, voice control, live translation and visual questions without pulling out a phone.

Main upgrade

Up to 3K video

Meta lists Gen 2 video capture at up to 3K Ultra HD. That makes Gen 2 more relevant for short-form creator footage than the older 1080p?-focused generation.

Battery

About 8 hours rated use

Meta advertises up to eight hours of typical use, with a charging case that provides additional charging on the go. Real-world endurance will vary with AI, camera, audio and connectivity use.

AI features

Meta AI + live translation

Current Meta pages highlight hands-free Meta AI, visual questions and live translation, but specific features and supported languages can vary by country.

September firmware update

v28 makes the glasses more useful away from the desk

The latest reported rollout adds practical everyday functions rather than changing the hardware.

Navigation

Hands-free walking directions

Firmware v28 adds voice-started pedestrian navigation in supported locations. Current reporting says users can ask the glasses to navigate to a destination, or share a destination from a phone mapping app to Meta AI. Availability is regional, so treat navigation as a location-dependent feature rather than a universal promise.

Audio

Spatial Audio for compatible Gen 2 models

The same update adds an optional Spatial Audio mode for music and podcasts on compatible Ray-Ban Meta Gen 2 optics and the newer Meta Glasses collection.

Language support

Mandarin support expands

v28 also adds Mandarin Chinese support across core controls, calls and messaging, capture, music and Meta AI functions according to current rollout reporting. Language availability can still differ by market.

Germany availability

More mainstream retail access

Deutsche Telekom began selling Ray-Ban Meta glasses in Germany on September 3, 2026, with prices from €419 and in-store viewing at participating shops. That makes fit checks easier for German buyers who do not want to order eyewear unseen.

What to consider

Important limitations before buying

AI glasses are more personal—and more privacy-sensitive—than a normal camera accessory.

Privacy

The capture LED has stronger anti-tamper protection

Meta says photos and video activate notification/capture LEDs and its current protections prohibit capture when the LED is covered. Recent reporting also confirms Meta closed a loophole that could allow recording to continue after the indicator was obscured. This is a meaningful privacy improvement, but it does not replace consent: follow local recording laws, avoid private settings and ask permission when appropriate.

Regional availability

Not every AI feature is universal

Translation languages, visual AI, navigation and other Meta AI functions can differ by market. Check the current support list in your country before buying primarily for one AI feature.

Creator use

Good POV camera, not a cinema camera

The glasses are convenient for spontaneous first-person clips, but a dedicated phone, action camera or mirrorless setup still gives creators more framing, stabilization, lens and audio control.

Fit + price

Try the frame, not only the features

Different frames, lenses and prescription options can change the final price substantially. Because this is eyewear, comfort and fit matter as much as the AI spec sheet.

Verdict

Who should consider Gen 2?

Gen 2 makes the most sense for someone who already wants smart glasses and values hands-free video, audio, navigation and AI enough to wear them regularly. If you mainly want translation or occasional AI questions, a phone or earbuds may deliver more value for less money.

Strong fit

POV creators, travelers, commuters and users who want open-ear audio plus camera-based Meta AI in one device.

Weak fit

Buyers who dislike camera wearables, need guaranteed offline AI, require professional video controls, or are buying only because “AI glasses” are trending.
