Plaud NotePin S vs NotePin — plus the new Plaud One AI earbuds

A research-led guide to Plaud's wearable conversation recorders, now updated for the newly announced Plaud One Explorer Edition.

Updated: August 29, 2026

Research status: This is not a hands-on test. Specifications and availability are based on Plaud's current product/support pages and current launch reporting. Confirm current details before buying.

Quick answer

NotePin S remains the safer buy today for someone who wants a compact wearable recorder that is already part of Plaud's established lineup. The original NotePin can still make sense at a meaningful discount. Plaud One is the product to watch if you want earbuds, calls, meetings and future agent-style workflows in one device—but it is an Explorer Edition shipping later, so AI Gear Hub is not treating it as a proven recommendation yet.

New: Plaud One Explorer Edition

Watchlist, not a full recommendation: Plaud One was announced on August 27, 2026 and the limited Explorer Edition is expected to begin shipping in Q4 2026. That means there is not yet enough real-world evidence for AI Gear Hub to rank it above established products.

Plaud One moves the idea from a pin into true-wireless earbuds and a recording case. Plaud lists three MEMS microphones plus a VPU in each earbud, four MEMS microphones with AI beamforming in the case, pickup up to 6.6 ft from the earbuds and 16.4 ft from the case, adaptive ANC up to 40 dB and a 12 mm dynamic driver.

The more important change is workflow ambition. Plaud says Plaud Agent can turn conversation context into notes, summaries, follow-ups and documents, with connected-tool actions where available. Availability of agent features, integrations and language support can vary by region and software version, so those future-facing capabilities should not be treated as guaranteed at launch.

What the Plaud ecosystem does

Recordings are managed through Plaud's app/web ecosystem, where supported AI features can turn conversations into transcripts, summaries and shareable outputs. Plaud advertises transcription and summaries in more than 112 languages, but accuracy varies with noise, speaker volume, language, accent and device position.

Reasons to consider NotePin S

  • Physical start/stop control is easy to understand.
  • Press-to-highlight marks important moments during a conversation.
  • Wearable form factor suits meetings, interviews, lectures and field notes.
  • It is an established shipping product rather than an Explorer preorder.

Important limitations

  • AI transcription and summaries depend on Plaud services rather than a completely offline workflow.
  • Heavy use can exceed included transcription or credit allowances.
  • NotePin S is aimed at in-person recording; Plaud One is the model designed to span more call scenarios.
  • Recording and consent laws vary by country and situation.

AI Gear Hub take

Buy NotePin S if you need a Plaud wearable now and value simple physical controls. Buy the older NotePin only when the discount is worthwhile. Wait on Plaud One unless you specifically want to participate in an early Explorer release; its combination of earbuds, standalone recording and agent workflows is genuinely interesting, but shipping, software maturity and ongoing credit costs still need real-world validation.
