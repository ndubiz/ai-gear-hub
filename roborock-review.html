Roborock Robot Vacuum Review: Is AI Cleaning Worth It?

What AI-powered mapping and obstacle avoidance actually change about daily cleaning.

Roborock's robot vacuums use AI-driven navigation — LiDAR mapping combined with computer-vision obstacle recognition — to clean a home methodically instead of bumping around at random like older robot vacuums. Most current models also mop, empty their own dustbin, and let you set no-go zones and cleaning schedules from an app.

What It Actually Does

On first run, the robot maps your home's layout room by room. The AI vision system identifies obstacles — cables, shoes, pet waste in some models — and routes around them rather than colliding and getting stuck. You can set specific rooms to clean, adjust suction per room type (carpet vs. hardwood), and in higher-end models, let it empty its own bin into a base station for weeks at a time.

What's good

  • Genuinely thorough, methodical cleaning vs. older random-path robots
  • Obstacle avoidance meaningfully reduces getting stuck or damaging items
  • App-based room selection and no-go zones are easy to set up
  • Self-emptying models cut hands-on maintenance to occasional bag changes

Where it falls short

  • Higher-end self-emptying models are a significant investment
  • Mopping function is a light pass, not a substitute for hand-mopping stains
  • Thick, high-pile rugs can still trip up navigation or reduce suction efficiency
  • Requires clear floors on the first mapping run to build an accurate map

Who It's For

Best fit: households with mostly hard flooring or low-pile carpet, pet owners wanting daily fur pickup without manual vacuuming, and anyone who wants cleaning to just happen on a schedule. If your home has thick shag carpet or a lot of ground clutter, a stick vacuum may still be more practical for now.

Our Verdict

Roborock's AI navigation is the real differentiator here — it's the difference between a vacuum that cleans deliberately and one that cleans by accident. For the right home layout, it removes a recurring chore almost entirely.
